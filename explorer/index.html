How the numbers are made

Value = height of the surface above the reference, measured along the normal of the reference ellipsoid (WGS 84 when the reference is a geoid). Latitude and longitude are geodetic on that ellipsoid. Red: surface above reference. Blue: below.

Geoids: NGA EGM96 (15′ grid) and EGM2008 (2.5′ grid, shown here at 7.5′, every third node), from PROJ-data (us_nga_egm96_15.tif, us_nga_egm08_25.tif). Both give geoid height above WGS 84. Values are stored to 1 cm and interpolated bilinearly between nodes. Thinning EGM2008 to 7.5′ changes values by 2 cm RMS (maximum 0.7 m) relative to the full grid.

The geoid comparisons are between models defined on the same ellipsoid (WGS 84), so the same latitude and longitude is the same point. Differences between WGS 84 realizations, at the centimetre level, are ignored.

The ellipsoids under “shape only” are placed with the WGS 84 centre and axes, so only their shape differs. Real datums built on them (NAD27, ED50, OSGB36, …) are also shifted and rotated relative to WGS 84, and a point's latitude and longitude change between datums. This page does not show those transformations; PROJ does them.

Coastlines and borders: Natural Earth 1:50m, via the world-atlas package.
